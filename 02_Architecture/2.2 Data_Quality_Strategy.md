# Data Quality Strategy

## Why Data Quality Matters

One of the biggest challenges in any supply chain environment is the quality of the underlying data.

Inventory planners, procurement teams, and business leaders make decisions based on the information available to them. If that information is incomplete, inconsistent, or inaccurate, it can result in poor decisions, inventory shortages, increased costs, and reduced customer satisfaction.

For this reason, data quality is treated as a core component of the platform rather than an afterthought.

The goal is not to assume perfect data. The goal is to identify issues early, make them visible, and ensure decision makers understand the reliability of the information being used.

---

# Common Data Quality Challenges

In a real-world environment, data often arrives from multiple operational systems with different standards and levels of quality.

Examples of issues that may occur include:

### Inventory Data

- Duplicate inventory records
- Negative inventory balances
- Missing branch information
- Delayed inventory updates

### Demand Data

- Duplicate demand transactions
- Missing quantities
- Invalid transaction dates
- Incorrect part references

### Purchase Order Data

- Missing receipt dates
- Late delivery updates
- Partial receipts
- Invalid supplier references

### Supplier Data

- Duplicate supplier names
- Supplier naming inconsistencies
- Missing lead times
- Outdated supplier information

### Master Data

- Missing product categories
- Invalid part numbers
- Incorrect branch assignments
- Duplicate records

# Data Quality Principles

The platform follows several key principles when evaluating data quality.

## Completeness

Required information should be present before records are used for decision making.

Examples:

- Missing Part Numbers
- Missing Supplier IDs
- Missing Branch IDs
- Missing Transaction Dates

---

## Validity

Data should conform to expected formats and business rules.

Examples:

- Future receipt dates that are unrealistic
- Negative quantities where not allowed
- Invalid part number formats

---

## Consistency

The same business entity should be represented consistently across systems.

Examples:

```text
ABC Industrial
ABC Industries
ABC IND
```

These may refer to the same supplier and should be standardized.

---

## Uniqueness

Business transactions should not be duplicated.

Examples:

- Duplicate inventory records
- Duplicate purchase orders
- Duplicate demand transactions

---

## Integrity

Relationships between datasets should remain valid.

Examples:

- Every demand transaction should reference a valid part
- Every purchase order should reference a valid supplier
- Every inventory record should reference a valid branch

---

# Data Quality Monitoring

The platform will continuously evaluate incoming data against predefined business rules.

When issues are detected, records will be flagged and tracked for review.

The purpose is not to silently remove data, but to make quality issues visible and measurable.

Metrics will include:

- Total Records Processed
- Data Quality Score
- Failed Records
- Critical Issues
- Duplicate Records
- Missing Values
- Invalid Records

---

# Data Quality Agent

The Data Quality Agent is responsible for identifying and prioritizing issues across all source systems.

The agent will:

- Monitor incoming data
- Detect rule violations
- Classify issues by severity
- Recommend corrective actions
- Provide visibility into data quality trends

The Data Quality Agent will act as the first layer of defense before information is used by forecasting models, inventory recommendations, and executive reporting.

---

# Success Criteria

The platform will be successful if users can:

- Trust the information used for decisions
- Identify data issues quickly
- Understand the impact of data quality problems
- Resolve critical issues before they affect business operations

Ultimately, data quality is not just a technical concern. It is a business capability that directly impacts the quality of decisions made across the supply chain.
