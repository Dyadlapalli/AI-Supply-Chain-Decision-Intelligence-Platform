# Case Study: The Same Supplier Under Different Names

## Summary

The supplier master holds **46 supplier records for 36 real suppliers**. Ten suppliers exist twice, under a second ID with a slightly different name, and **$317,855 of purchase order spend across 302 PO lines** is booked to those duplicate IDs. Another **$33,564 (37 lines)** points to a supplier ID that does not exist at all.

Left unresolved, every supplier KPI (spend, on-time delivery, lead time) is split across two records, and the Supplier Risk Agent would evaluate one real supplier as two smaller, unrelated ones.

This case study shows how the issue was found, why simple de-duplication misses it, and how the platform resolves it.

---

# How It Was Found

A first look at the raw supplier master in SQL Server:

```sql
SELECT supplier_id, supplier_name, supplier_tier, lead_time_days
FROM raw.master_supplier_master
WHERE supplier_name LIKE '%CAT%' OR supplier_name LIKE '%Caterpillar%'
ORDER BY supplier_name;
```

| supplier_id | supplier_name | supplier_tier | lead_time_days |
|---|---|---|---|
| S103 | Cat Parts Dist Ctr - Atlanta | Tier 1 | 3 |
| S117 | Cat Parts Dist Ctr - Denver | Tier 1 | 4 |
| S004 | Caterpillar Inc. - Machine Order Fulfillment | Tier 1 | 45 |
| S104 | CATERPILLAR INC. - MACHINE ORDER FULFILLMENT | Tier 1 | 45 |
| S003 | Caterpillar Inc. - Parts Distribution Center Atlanta | Tier 1 | 3 |
| S017 | Caterpillar Inc. - Parts Distribution Center Denver | Tier 1 | 4 |
| ... | | | |

Three of Caterpillar's own facilities appear twice. A wider check found ten in total.

---

# Why Simple De-duplication Misses It

`SELECT DISTINCT supplier_name` treats every one of these as a different supplier. The variations are the kind that build up in any real master file:

| Pattern | Original | Duplicate |
|---|---|---|
| Abbreviation | Caterpillar Inc. - Parts Distribution Center Atlanta | Cat Parts Dist Ctr - Atlanta |
| Capitalization | Caterpillar Inc. - Machine Order Fulfillment | CATERPILLAR INC. - MACHINE ORDER FULFILLMENT |
| Legal suffix | Perkins Engines | Perkins Engines LLC |
| Punctuation | Blue Ridge Industrial Supply | Blue Ridge Industrial Supply, Inc. |
| **Invisible trailing space** | Bridgestone OTR | `Bridgestone OTR␣` |

The trailing-space case is the most dangerous: the two names look identical on screen, in Excel, and in Power BI, but they are different values to every join and `GROUP BY`. Three suppliers (Eaton Hydraulics, Bridgestone OTR, Appalachian Fastener) are duplicated this way.

The pairs also disagree on attributes that drive planning, so "which record is right?" has no obvious answer:

| Supplier | Original | Duplicate |
|---|---|---|
| Perkins Engines | Lead time 35 days | Lead time missing |
| Bosch Rexroth | Lead time `20 days` (text, unusable) | Lead time 20, but marked **inactive** while 27 PO lines still reference it |

# Business Impact

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Real suppliers | 36 |
| Supplier records in the master | 46 (10 duplicates) |
| PO lines booked to a duplicate supplier ID | 302 |
| Spend booked to a duplicate supplier ID | $317,855 |
| PO lines referencing a non-existent supplier (`S099`) | 37 ($33,564) |
| Supplier exception list rows identifying the supplier by free text only | 31 |

What this breaks downstream:

- **Supplier spend and leverage.** Spend with a supplier is understated, which weakens negotiating position and hides concentration risk.
- **On-time delivery (SUP-01).** Late deliveries booked to the duplicate ID are invisible in the original supplier's score, so a struggling supplier can look healthy.
- **Lead time planning (SUP-02, INV-06).** Safety stock and reorder points use the master lead time. Two conflicting records mean two different answers.
- **AI recommendations.** The Supplier Risk Agent would assess one supplier as two small, low-risk ones, the opposite of a correct escalation.

---

# Resolution Approach

This is a **master data management** problem: resolve every record to one surviving "golden" supplier and keep a map from every duplicate ID to it.

1. **Normalize names.** Uppercase, trim spaces (including trailing), remove punctuation and legal suffixes (`INC`, `LLC`, `CO`), expand known abbreviations (`DIST CTR` → `DISTRIBUTION CENTER`, `CAT` → `CATERPILLAR`).
2. **Match.** Records with the same normalized name and the same city and state are the same supplier.
3. **Choose the survivor.** The original (lowest) ID survives. Attributes come from the survivor unless it is missing a value the duplicate has.
4. **Map, don't delete.** A cross-reference table `clean.supplier_xref` maps every source ID (`S103`) to its golden ID (`S003`). Purchase orders and receipts are re-pointed through this map, so their history is kept and counts toward the right supplier.
5. **Flag what cannot be matched.** References to IDs that exist nowhere (`S099`) are not guessed. They are logged as Integrity issues for the Data Steward and linked to the Unknown supplier member.
6. **Verify.** The result is checked against the platform's data quality answer key (`dq_issue_log.csv`): every injected duplicate must be resolved, with no false merges.
